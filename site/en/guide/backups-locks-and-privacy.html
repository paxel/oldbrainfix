Backups are encrypted files of everything on a Device, opened with your passphrase or with the recovery key on the Recovery Sheet. The App Lock and the Area Locks keep others from browsing your Notes on the phone, and a block keeps a person's writing off your Devices. Each box below shows the steps on the phone and on the desktop.

The desktop app has its Backups in a dialog under Sync ▸ Backups…. It has no App Lock and no Area Locks. Block and Unblock are at the end of a person's Profile.

Set up Backups

Make encrypted Backups possible, with a passphrase and a Recovery Sheet.

  • Tap your Profile picture on the Agenda.
  • Tap Back up this Device.
  • Type a passphrase into both fields.
  • Tap Set up Backups.
  • Tap Print or save and keep the sheet somewhere safe.
    •
  • Click Sync ▸ Backups….
  • Type a passphrase into both fields.
  • Click Set up Backups.
  • Choose where to save the Recovery Sheet.
  • Print the sheet and keep it somewhere safe.
    •

    On the Profile page, under Backups and recovery, Back up this Device opens Backups. "A Backup is an encrypted file of all your Areas, Notes, photos, voice recordings, Routines and their history. Only your passphrase, or the recovery key on the Recovery Sheet, opens it. This Device's settings stay out, except the playback speed. All your Devices share one passphrase and one recovery key, and each writes Backups of its own." The two settings of the person, Simple or Advanced and "Move a Note to the Trash when its one-time Reminder is done", are not settings of the Device: they sync to your own Devices and come with a Backup.

    The passphrase has at least 8 characters, typed into Passphrase and again into Passphrase again; a short sentence is easy to remember. Set up Backups takes a few seconds. Set up on another of your Devices already, they need no setting up here: sync with that Device, and this one takes the same passphrase and recovery key. While a sync runs, a thin line moves under the top bar, on the desktop under the menu bar.

    The Recovery Sheet is a PDF page with your recovery key, as text and as a QR code. The recovery key opens the Backups of all your Devices, also when the passphrase is forgotten, so whoever holds it can read them: keep the sheet somewhere safe, in a drawer or a password manager, never next to the Backups. Print or save opens the print dialog, Share hands the PDF to the app you pick. The Backups page offers both at any time, not only after setting up. The sheet also says how to restore.

    On the desktop, Set up Backups asks at once where to save the Recovery Sheet; Save the Recovery Sheet… saves it again at any time. Print it from there.

    A Backup is restored on the first start of a Device that holds no Notes yet, with Restore from a Backup (see Restore from a Backup), on the phone and on the desktop: choose the Backup file, then type the passphrase, or the recovery key from the Recovery Sheet; the phone can also scan it. Settings such as alarms, Quiet Hours and locks are not in a Backup; set them again afterwards, choose the Backup folder again and pair your other Devices again. Simple or Advanced and the Trash setting come back with the Backup.

    On the phone, Open a Backup… under Take Notes back on the Backups page brings Notes back from a Backup without restoring it, such as a Note deleted for good or an earlier version of a list. On an Android phone the file picker starts in the Backups folder; then type the passphrase, or the recovery key, or scan it. The page shows the Backup's Notes as the tree of a Pack, under the date the Backup was made, and a Note that lay in its Trash says "In the Trash". The Notes of a locked Area that is closed are left out; Unlock and show them asks for the PIN and adds them to the tree. No Note is ticked at first; a Note ticked comes back with its photos, recordings and Routines. Add takes the ticked Notes in as copies in new Areas named after the Backup's, such as "Doctor (Backup)" when a Doctor Area is here already, and the Notes of no Area in "From a Backup". As with a Pack, a copy comes without its Tally entries, ticks, check-offs and history; nothing else on the Device changes.

    Back up every week

    Have a Backup written into a folder of your choice every week.

  • Tap your Profile picture on the Agenda.
  • Tap Back up this Device.
  • Tap Choose folder and pick a folder.
  • Leave Back up every week switched on.
    •
  • Click Sync ▸ Backups….
  • Click Choose folder and pick a folder.
  • Leave Back up every week ticked.
    •

    "Backups go into a folder you choose: on this Device, on a USB stick or in a cloud app." Choose folder picks it and writes the first Backup into it at once. Back up every week is on by default: "The last 4 Backups of this Device are kept; older ones go." Backups of your other Devices in the same folder are left alone. Back up now writes one at once. The page shows when the last Backup was written, or that the last one could not be written, with the question whether the folder is still there. Each Backup is a file named after the time it was written, ending in .obfbackup.

    The phone writes the weekly Backup when the app comes into view and in its work in the background, once a week has passed since the last one. The desktop writes it for as long as the app runs, also in the tray; only on Linux without a tray does the app end with its window.

    On an Android phone, Backups also offer Also keep the newest Backup in my Google account, off by default. "Android's own backup then keeps the newest Backup file, encrypted as it is, in your Google account, and nothing else of the app. On a new phone it comes back with your apps, and the app asks for the passphrase or the recovery key." With it on, the weekly Backup needs no folder. Google takes at most 25 MB: a larger Backup is not kept there, and the page says so; a folder still takes it. The desktop has no Google backup.

    Lock the app with a PIN

    Ask for a PIN on the phone before your Notes show.

  • Tap your Profile picture, then Settings.
  • Switch on App Lock under Privacy.
  • Type a PIN of 4 to 12 digits, twice.
  • Pick a time under Lock again after.
    •
  • Lock the computer's own screen when you leave it.
    •

    The desktop has no App Lock: whoever uses the computer's open user account sees every Note in its window. The App Lock, the Area Locks and the PIN are settings of the phone they were set on: they are not synced and not in a Backup.

    Settings, under Privacy: App Lock "asks for the PIN before showing your Notes. Capture and alarms never need it." While the app is locked, it shows "Old Brain Fix is locked" with the PIN field and Unlock; its "+" fans out the ways to capture as on the Agenda, without unlocking.

    The PIN has 4 to 12 digits and is asked for twice when a lock is first switched on. It unlocks the App Lock and the Area Locks on this phone. Change PIN changes it. Unlock with fingerprint or screen lock shows once a PIN is set, where the phone has them; switching it on asks for the PIN first. The PIN always works as well.

    Lock again after sets how long the app may be in the background before it locks again: At once, or after 1, 5 or 15 minutes; 1 minute by default. Turning a lock off and changing the PIN ask for the PIN or the fingerprint first.

    Behind the App Lock, a Sent Item or a Profile card file opened from another app and a tap on the Changes Nudge wait until the app is unlocked.

    On the iPhone the switch is Unlock with Face ID or Touch ID; the iPhone's passcode works when they fail.

    Hide an Area behind the PIN

    Keep one Area and its Notes hidden on the phone until you unlock it.

  • Tap your Profile picture, then Settings.
  • Tap Area Locks under Privacy.
  • Switch on the lock of the Area.
  • Choose Masked or In full for its alarms.
    •
  • Lock the computer's own screen when you leave it.
    •

    The desktop has no Area Locks, so nothing on it is Masked: an Area locked on the phone shows on the desktop in full, with its Notes, its Routines and their notifications. The locks and the PIN are settings of the phone they were set on: they are not synced and not in a Backup.

    Under the App Lock, Area Locks lists every Area with its switch. "A locked Area and its Notes stay hidden until you unlock it." The App Lock need not be on; switching on the first lock asks for a PIN when none is set. In the Areas list, a locked Area asks for the PIN or the fingerprint when it is opened; it stays open until the app locks again or you are back on the Agenda. Unlock {Area} for good asks the same before a lock goes off.

    While an Area is locked, "Alarms and Agenda show" chooses:

    • Masked, the default: its Occurrences show in notifications and on the Agenda as "Private Routine, 08:00" or "Private Reminder", without the Note's text, and can be checked off all the same. On the Agenda, a Frog, a Shopping List and a Checklist of a Masked Area show as "Private Frog", "Private Shopping List" and "Private Checklist", and a Session's pick from it as "Private Tally".
    • In full: they show as for any other Area. Choosing it asks for the PIN or the fingerprint.

    The Agenda widget on the home screen shows a Masked Area's rows as the Agenda does. In a Note's History, an Area or Topic of a locked Area that is not open is named "A locked Area", never by its name. A Routine of a Masked Area written into your calendar shows there as "Private Routine". A Note of a locked Area is never handed to another app while the Area is closed.

    Block a person

    Keep everything a person writes off your Devices.

  • Tap your Profile picture on the Agenda.
  • Tap the person under People.
  • Tap Block on the bar.
  • Tap Block in the dialog.
    •
  • Click Sync ▸ People, then the person.
  • Click Block at the end of their Profile.
  • Click Block in the dialog.
    •

    On another person's Profile, Block sits on the bar, on the desktop at the end of the Profile; a Sent Item's or a shared Area's preview offers Decline and block. "Everything {name} wrote is deleted from your Devices, except their check-offs, and nothing more of theirs is kept. A shared folder can still hold their encrypted files, which the app never opens; leaving the folder removes them."

    • The block goes to all of your own Devices. Nothing they write afterwards is kept or shown, in any Shared Area and from any Device that passes it on, and no Sent Item of theirs is shown. Their check-offs stay, without their name or picture.
    • The person is not told.
    • Blocking a Manager of a Shared Area you are in means leaving that Area: the dialog names the Areas and offers Block and leave. What the Area held stays on your Devices.
    • Blocked people leave People and are listed in the fold Blocked under it, without their picture. Unblock on their Profile: "What they wrote comes back with the next sync with a Device that still holds it." To take what they send, unblock them, then ask them to send it again.
    • Notes you accepted from them as Sent Items before the block stay.

    See what the app keeps where

    Know which Devices hold your Notes and settings, and what leaves them.

  • Tap your Profile picture, then Settings.
  • Read the line under the search field on what syncs.
  • Tap About Old Brain Fix at the end.
  • Tap Privacy policy.
    •
  • Click Edit ▸ Settings… and read the line under the search field.
  • Click Help ▸ About Old Brain Fix.
  • Click Privacy policy.
    •

    Everything you capture stays on your own Devices: on the Device you captured it on, on the Devices you paired with it, and, for a Note in a Shared Area, on the Devices of that Area's people. There is no server and no account. The privacy policy lists what the app stores, when it goes online and what each permission is used for.

    Most settings stay on the Device they were set on. "Simple or Advanced and the Trash setting sync to your own Devices; the other settings belong to this phone." These two are settings of you as a person: they go to your own Devices and into your Backups, never to anyone else. On the phone and the desktop, the search field above that line finds a setting by its name or detail line; a search that finds nothing says so. On the desktop Ctrl+F puts the cursor into it.

    The app sends nothing on its own. When the phone app stopped unexpectedly, the next start says so at the top, and a tap opens what happened, with Close and Report. Report opens your mail app with that text to the developer, to send from there; Close sends nothing. A failure shown at the top, on the phone or the desktop, offers the same.

    On the iPhone, the phone's own backup to iCloud or a computer leaves out your Notes, photos, recordings and settings in Old Brain Fix. Your Backups, in the folder you chose, are the copy to restore from.

    Send feedback

    Tell the developer what happened or what could be better, and see everything before it goes.

  • Tap your Profile picture, then Settings.
  • Tap Send feedback at the end.
  • Write what happened, and tick what goes along.
  • Read the preview, then tap Mail or Share….
    •
  • Click Edit ▸ Settings…, then Send feedback.
  • Write what happened, and tick what goes along.
  • Read the preview, then click Mail or Save as file….
    •

    The version, the system and the device, and where the app was installed from (Play, F-Droid, an APK, Flatpak, Homebrew, Scoop and the like) are ticked at first; each shows its value under its tick. The last crash report, on the phone, and Notes as a Pack are not. The preview shows exactly the text that is sent, and nothing is sent until you tap Mail or Share… and send it from the app you picked.

    • Mail opens your mail app with the text to the developer. A mail link carries about 1800 characters; a longer text goes through Share…, on the desktop as a file.
    • Notes as a Pack opens the Pack's tree of every Area, nothing ticked; the Notes you tick go along as a Pack file. A mail link cannot carry it: on the phone Share… hands the text and the Pack to the app you pick, a mail app with the developer's address filled in; on the desktop Save the Pack… saves it to attach to the mail.
    • A locked Area that is not open has no Notes in the tree.

    On the iPhone, where the app was installed from reads "App Store", "TestFlight" or "unknown".
