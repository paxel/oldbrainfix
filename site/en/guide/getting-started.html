On its first start, Old Brain Fix asks how it starts on this Device: new, as another Device of yours, or from a Backup. Each box below shows the steps on the phone and on the desktop.

The desktop app's first start offers the same three choices, each a button under its explanation.

Old Brain Fix for the iPhone, for iOS 26 and newer, is for testers for now, through Apple's TestFlight app. It works as on an Android phone; where the iPhone differs, a box's details say so.

Start new

Start with an empty app and the Areas you pick.

  • Tap New to Old Brain Fix.
  • Pick Simple or Advanced.
  • Untick the Areas and Topics you don't need.
  • Tap Start.
    •
  • Click New to Old Brain Fix.
  • Pick Simple or Advanced.
  • Untick the Areas and Topics you don't need.
  • Click Start.
    •

    Simple offers making Notes, Checklists, Shopping Lists, Reminders and Routines. Advanced adds Tallies, Sessions, Frogs, Topics, the format bar and roles in Shared Areas. Settings changes it any time; it is the same on all your own Devices, and what you made stays either way; the Topics ticked below it are planted in Simple too. Someone who updated from an earlier version and never chose has Advanced.

    The app adds the Area Old Brain Fix with a Quick start Note, a Checklist of steps to try, and What the icons mean.

    The Starter Set, every Area and Topic ticked:

    • Household with Kitchen, Garden and Cleaning
    • Work with Meetings and To sort out
    • Car with Service and Repairs
    • Health with Doctor and Medication

    A Topic stays only with its Area: unticking an Area unticks its Topics, and ticking a Topic ticks its Area again. Start keeps what is ticked. Back on the phone and Cancel on the desktop return to the three choices. Areas and Topics can be renamed, added and deleted later in the notes browser; on the desktop, more Areas are made with the "+" next to Areas in the sidebar.

    On the phone your Profile is made from the Device's name; its name and picture are changed on the Profile page, behind the Profile picture in the Agenda's top corner. On the desktop the Profile takes the name and picture of your login on the computer, or else the computer's name.

    On the iPhone the Profile is called "iPhone" at first, as iOS tells apps no other name for the phone; change it on the Profile page.

    Add a Device of mine

    Bring everything over from another Device of yours that runs Old Brain Fix.

  • Tap Add a Device of mine.
  • Open Sync on your other Device and show its code.
  • Tap Scan the code, or type it into Pair code.
  • Tap Sync now.
    •
  • Click Add a Device of mine.
  • Scan or type this computer's code on your other Device.
  • Click Trust when that Device asks.
    •

    The two Devices pair either way round. On the phone, Show this Device's code shows the new phone's code for the other Device to scan. On the desktop, the page shows this computer's code as a QR code and as text with its PIN, with Copy the code; Refuse turns a Device away. Under "Sync with a Device that shows its code", type or paste the code another Device shows into Pair code and click Sync with this code.

    Once a sync went through, the new Device holds your Profile, your Areas and your Notes. The Starter Set is not offered, and the Profile is the one of your other Device. A failed sync, a Device turned away or Back return to the three choices; on the desktop also Cancel, Alt+Left or the mouse's back button.

    Restore from a Backup

    Bring everything back from a Backup file onto a Device that holds nothing yet.

  • Tap Restore from a Backup.
  • Choose the Backup file.
  • Type the passphrase, or the recovery key from the Recovery Sheet.
  • Tap Restore.
    •
  • Click Restore from a Backup.
  • Choose the Backup file in the system's file dialog.
  • Type the passphrase, or the recovery key from the Recovery Sheet.
  • Click Restore.
    •

    The row shows only on a Device that holds nothing yet and can pick a file. A Backup goes only onto a Device that holds no Notes yet. On the phone the recovery key can also be scanned from the sheet. The Starter Set is not offered afterwards: the Areas come with the Backup.

    A Backup holds Simple or Advanced and the Trash setting, but no other settings. After a restore, set alarms and locks again, choose the Backup folder again and pair your other Devices again.

    When Android's own backup brought a Backup back from your Google account together with the app, the first start opens it at once ("A Backup came back") and asks for the passphrase or the recovery key. Start without it leaves it and returns to the three choices.

    On the iPhone, the phone's own backup to iCloud or a computer leaves out your Notes and settings in Old Brain Fix, so a new iPhone set up from it starts the app empty: restore from a Backup file, or add it as a Device of yours.

    Import notes from another app

    Turn a folder of Markdown or text files into Notes.

  • Tap your Profile picture, then Settings of this Device.
  • Type Import into the search field at the top.
  • Tap Import a folder.
  • Pick the folder.
  • Tap Import finished. at the top to see what came in.
    •
  • Click File ▸ Import….
  • Click A folder… and pick the folder.
  • Click Import finished. at the top to see what came in.
    •

    Each file ending in .md, .markdown or .txt, in the folder and in its folders except hidden ones, becomes one Note: Markdown stays formatted and a text file plain, and the file's name becomes the Note's first line unless the text already begins with it. Each Note is dated when its file was last changed.

    All of them land in a new Area named after the folder, such as "From Notes"; importing the same folder again makes a second Area, "From Notes (2)". None of them is Unsorted, and no Nudge comes for them. Other files, hidden files and files too large for a Note (more than 1 MiB) are skipped. While the folder is read, the row or the Import dialog says "Reading the folder…". What "Import finished." shows: how many Notes went into which Area and how many files were skipped and why, with Open the Area. A folder without a Markdown or text file changes nothing. After the Import the app keeps no access to the folder, unless it is the shared folder or the Backup folder.

    Import notes from Google Keep

    Turn an export of Google Keep from Google Takeout into Notes.

  • Export Keep alone on takeout.google.com.
  • Tap your Profile picture, then Settings of this Device.
  • Type Import into the search field at the top.
  • Tap Import from Google Keep.
  • Tap Zip file or Folder and pick the export.
    •
  • Export Keep alone on takeout.google.com.
  • Click File ▸ Import…, then Google Keep….
  • Click Zip file or Folder and pick the export.
    •

    On takeout.google.com, deselect all products, select Keep alone and create the export; Google then offers a zip file to download. Zip file opens the system's file picker, Folder its folder picker for a folder unpacked from the zip. The zip is read as it is, so it need not be unpacked.

    Each Keep note becomes one Note in a new Area, "From Google Keep", dated when it was made in Keep, with its title as its first line and its labels as #tags on a last line. A list becomes a Checklist with its Lines checked as in Keep; Checklists stay off the Agenda until you switch them on with Show on the Agenda. A note's photos come with it as its photos.

    Archived notes come over as any other; notes in Keep's trash are skipped, and so are voice recordings, attachments the export lacks and files too large (a note of more than 1 MiB, a photo of more than 16 MiB). Pinned notes and colours are left out, and no Nudge comes for any of the Notes. While the export is read, the row or the Import dialog says "Reading the export…"; then "Import finished." at the top shows how many Notes, Checklists and photos went into the Area and what was skipped and why, with Open the Area. After the Import the app keeps no access to the zip file or the folder.

    Set up alarms and voice Notes

    Let Routines and Reminders ring on time, and voice Notes become text.

  • Tap Download for the speech model, or Not now.
  • Tap Allow for each access the alarms need.
  • Tap Next to go on.
    •
  • Click Edit ▸ Settings….
  • Keep Notifications on this computer ticked.
  • Keep Start at login ticked.
  • Download a speech model under Speech model.
    •

    After the Starter Set, a restore or an added Device, the phone asks "Transcribe voice Notes on the phone?". The speech model turns voice recordings into a Transcript you can read and search; it is downloaded once (148 MB) from Old Brain Fix's own release on GitHub and runs on the phone. Download starts the download in the background, and the setup goes on at once; on mobile data the screen says how much of it the download uses. The offer is made only once and only where the speech model can run and none is in place yet. Settings, Speech model offers it at any time later, with a choice between the base and the small model, or a file of your own. Recordings are kept either way.

    Last, "Set up alarms" walks through the access that Routines and Reminders need, one step at a time: Notifications, Alarms on the minute and Full-screen alarms. Allow opens the system's question or page; Next goes on, also without it. A red banner on the Agenda shows while one is missing and leads back to the setup.

    The desktop asks for neither the speech model nor alarm access on its first start; its Settings hold both. The first time you capture a Note, the app asks once whether it may record where you were ("Remember where you were?"). Capturing itself never asks anything.

    For bigger text and buttons, the phone's Settings have Fat fingers under "Look": text shows 1.3 times its size on top of the system's font size, and icons and tap targets 1.3 times theirs, on the Agenda and in its widget too. The desktop's Settings have Bigger text and icons under "This computer", which zooms the window to 1.3 times. Both are off at first and belong to the Device.

    On the iPhone, "Set up alarms" asks for Notifications and Alarms only. Alarms lets an escalated Routine ring as an iPhone alarm (see Routines, Reminders and the Agenda). The first Photo asks for the camera, and Paste may show iOS's own "Allow Paste" question before it reads the clipboard; both questions come from iOS, not from the app.
