Old Brain Fix has no sync over the internet of its own. Devices far apart sync through a shared folder that another app carries between them: Syncthing from device to device, or Autosync through a cloud such as Google Drive, Dropbox or OneDrive. Everything Old Brain Fix writes into the folder is encrypted. Each box below shows the steps on the phone and on the desktop. On the iPhone, iCloud Drive or Möbius Sync carries the folder.

Sync your own Devices with Syncthing

Keep your phone and your computer in sync wherever they are, without a cloud.

  • Install Syncthing-Fork from F-Droid or Google Play.
  • Add a folder in Syncthing-Fork and share it with your computer.
  • Tap your Profile picture, then Sync with another Device.
  • Tap Choose folder and pick the Syncthing folder.
    •
  • Install Syncthing and accept the folder the phone shares.
  • Click Sync ▸ Sync now.
  • Click Choose folder and pick the Syncthing folder.
    •

    Syncthing is open source and has no central server: it syncs the folder between your devices, without a cloud account.

    On Android, the official Syncthing app was discontinued in December 2024. Syncthing-Fork carries on from it and is updated; it is on F-Droid and on Google Play. On the computer, syncthing.net lists Syncthing for Linux, Windows and macOS: Linux distributions package it, and for Windows and macOS it names installers such as Syncthing Windows Setup, SyncTrayzor and syncthing-macos.

    On the iPhone, Möbius Sync, by Pickup Infinity, is a Syncthing app from the App Store. It is free for up to 20 MB of files in total; a one-time in-app purchase lifts the limit. Its folders show in the Files app, so Choose folder in Old Brain Fix can pick one. iOS lets Möbius Sync sync only while it is open, for a short time after, and when iOS wakes it; its FAQ says syncing may take 24 hours to start and then runs about 1 to 2 hours a day. Open Möbius Sync now and then to bring changes in sooner.

    Pair your Devices once in person first (see Sync with another Device of yours): files from a Device this one is not paired with are counted and left unread. Old Brain Fix keeps its files in a folder named oldbrainfix-sync inside the one you choose. Each Device chooses one shared folder; it carries your own Devices and every Shared Area that travels through a folder.

    Share an Area with your family through Syncthing

    Share one Area with a person far away through a Syncthing folder you both have.

  • Share your Syncthing folder with their Syncthing.
  • Tap ⋮ next to the Area, then Share….
  • Tap Share with… and pick the person.
  • Tap A shared folder you both reach, then Share.
  • Send the offer; they accept and choose the same folder.
    •
  • Share your Syncthing folder with their Syncthing.
  • Right-click the Area in the left pane, then click Share….
  • Click Share with… and click the person.
  • Pick A shared folder you both reach, then click Share.
  • Send the offer file; they accept and choose the same folder.
    •

    The person needs Old Brain Fix and Syncthing, and must be among your People (see Add a person). In Syncthing, add their device and share your folder with it; they accept it on their side. The offer itself goes as a file through any messenger, as in Share an Area with someone far away. When they accept it and have no shared folder yet, the app asks "Choose the shared folder?", and they pick the Syncthing folder.

    The folder is the same one your own Devices sync through, and it holds what all of them write, each Area sealed with a key of its own. The other person's app reads only the files of the Areas shared with them; everything else stays sealed. What the folder shows to anyone who has it is which Devices sync and how much, not what was written.

    Use a cloud you already share, with Autosync

    Keep the shared folder in a cloud your family already uses, such as Google Drive or Dropbox.

  • Install Autosync from Google Play.
  • Pair a folder on the phone with a folder in the cloud.
  • Tap your Profile picture, then Sync with another Device.
  • Tap Choose folder and pick the phone folder Autosync syncs.
    •
  • Install the cloud's desktop app, such as Dropbox or Nextcloud.
  • Click Sync ▸ Sync now.
  • Click Choose folder and pick a folder the cloud app syncs.
    •

    Autosync, by MetaCtrl, keeps a folder on the phone and a folder in the cloud the same in both directions: new files in the cloud come down, new files on the phone go up, and a file deleted on one side is deleted on the other.

    • Autosync - File Sync & Backup covers Google Drive, OneDrive, SharePoint Online, Dropbox, Box, MEGA, Nextcloud, ownCloud, pCloud, Yandex Disk, WebDAV, FTP, SFTP and network drives. It starts as a trial and then asks for a purchase.
    • Autosync for Google Drive, Dropsync (Dropbox), OneSync (OneDrive), MegaSync and BoxSync each cover one cloud. They are free with ads, for one pair of folders and uploads up to 10 MB; the premium version, an in-app purchase, adds more pairs, larger files and no ads.

    A recording or photo larger than 10 MB needs the premium version of a single-cloud app. For a Shared Area, each person syncs the same folder of the cloud, for example one shared with the others inside the cloud, and chooses it in Old Brain Fix as in Share an Area with your family through Syncthing.

    On the computer, the clouds' own desktop apps sync a real folder: the Nextcloud desktop app on Linux, Windows and macOS, the Dropbox folder in File Explorer or Finder and on Linux, and Google Drive for desktop, which mirrors My Drive into a folder on Windows and macOS.

    On the iPhone, iCloud Drive needs no other app: tap Choose folder and pick a folder in iCloud Drive. A Mac reaches the same folder in the Finder, and a Windows PC with iCloud for Windows, which keeps iCloud Drive in C:\Users\<name>\iCloud Drive. Apple has no iCloud Drive app for Android or Linux, only iCloud.com in the browser, which keeps no folder in sync: with an Android phone or a Linux computer, use Syncthing, with Möbius Sync on the iPhone.

    Old Brain Fix on the iPhone syncs through the folder while it is open. In the background it syncs only when iOS gives it time, and iOS decides when that is.

    Why the Google Drive app is not enough

    The phone apps of Google Drive, Dropbox and Nextcloud keep no folder in two-way sync.

  • Use Autosync or Syncthing-Fork instead of the cloud's own app.
  • Tap Choose folder and pick the folder that app syncs.
    •
  • Install the cloud's desktop app.
  • Click Sync ▸ Sync now.
  • Click Choose folder and pick a folder inside its synced folder.
    •

    The shared folder works only when files the other Devices write appear in it on their own, and files Old Brain Fix writes leave it on their own. The cloud apps on the phone do not do that:

    • Google Drive: the Android app makes single files available offline with "Make available offline"; its help describes no folder on the phone kept in sync.
    • Dropbox: the Android app makes a whole folder available offline only on paid plans, and by default offline files are not kept up to date over mobile data.
    • Nextcloud: its user manual says that two-way sync has not yet been fully implemented in the Android client.

    On the computer this is different: the desktop apps of Nextcloud, Dropbox and Google Drive keep a real folder in sync, and Old Brain Fix on the desktop can use a folder inside it.
