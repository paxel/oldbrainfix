# Capture with a hardware button

Old Brain Fix offers three capture screens that open directly, with nothing
in between: **Voice note**, **Text note** and **Photo note**. You reach them

- as **app shortcuts**: long-press the Old Brain Fix icon,
- as **Quick Settings tiles**: edit the tiles in the notification shade and
  add "Voice note", "Text note" or "Photo note",
- as **home-screen widgets**: one button per mode, or all three side by
  side,
- as **activities** that other apps can start: "Voice note", "Text note"
  and "Photo note" under Old Brain Fix.

A phone's own key or gesture can start any of these. What a phone offers
depends on its brand, model and Android version; the menus below are where
the setting sits on many models, not on all of them. Menu names may differ
on yours.

## While the phone is locked

Capture screens are part of the app, and the app's Notes stay behind the
phone's lock: a capture screen shows only once the phone is unlocked. A tile
tapped on the lock screen asks you to unlock right away, then opens the
capture screen. Old Brain Fix's own App Lock never stands in front of
Capture.

## Samsung

On many Samsung models, **Settings › Advanced features › Side button** lets a
double press of the side key open an app of your choice. Picked there, Old
Brain Fix opens on its home screen, not on a capture screen. To land on a
capture screen directly, use a button-mapper app (see below) with one of the
shortcuts.

## Motorola

On many Motorola models, the **Moto** app (or **Settings › Gestures**) has
gestures such as a double tap on the back of the phone that can open an app.
Where the gesture offers app shortcuts, pick "Voice note", "Text note" or
"Photo note"; otherwise use a button-mapper app.

## Xiaomi, Redmi, POCO

On many models, **Settings › Additional settings › Gesture shortcuts** (on
some models a back tap) can start an app. Where it offers app shortcuts,
pick one of Old Brain Fix's; otherwise use a button-mapper app.

## Nothing

On many Nothing models with the Essential Key, that key is kept for
Nothing's own features.
Use the tiles, the widgets or the shortcuts instead, or a button-mapper app
for another key, such as a volume key.

## Google Pixel

Pixel phones have no extra button for this. Put a Quick Settings tile or a
widget where you reach it fastest, or long-press the app icon for the
shortcuts. On many Pixel models, **Settings › System › Gestures › Quick
Tap** (a double tap on the back) can open an app; it opens Old Brain Fix on
its home screen.

## Any phone: a button-mapper app

Button-mapper apps from the app store can map a key press (for example a
long press of a volume key) to an action. On many of them, the action can be
an app shortcut or an app's activity: choose Old Brain Fix's "Voice note",
"Text note" or "Photo note". Such apps usually need Android's accessibility
access to see key presses; that access is given to the mapper app, not to
Old Brain Fix.
